Operating Rhythm Kit

The meeting calendar and agendas I use to run planning and delivery for an engineering organization

An operating rhythm is a fixed set of meetings that happen on a predictable schedule. Everyone knows where decisions get made, where problems get raised and when status is shared. I run a rhythm like this for a 119-person engineering organization.

Agenda templates

Weekly scrum of scrums (30 min)

  1. Each team: what's next, what's blocked
  2. Dependencies between teams: who needs what from whom, and by when
  3. Risks to the current release

Weekly executive status (written, plus 15 min)

  1. Top 3 headlines this week
  2. Programs: green, yellow or red, with one line on why
  3. Risks and the plan for each
  4. Decisions or help needed from executives

Release readiness review (30 min)

  1. Scope: what's in, what was cut
  2. Quality: open defects by severity and test results
  3. Security: open vulnerabilities and any exceptions
  4. Readiness: documentation, support and sales enablement
  5. Go or no-go decision

Monthly planning (3 hrs)

  1. Last month: what was planned versus what was done
  2. Requests from Sales and customers, ranked
  3. Capacity: people available and work already committed
  4. Trade-offs: what we'll do, what we won't, and why
  5. Agreed priorities for next month

Quarterly big room planning (Over 2 days)

  1. Company goals for the quarter, from leadership
  2. Teams plan their work in breakout sessions
  3. Dependency mapping: teams agree on hand-offs face to face
  4. Risks: each one is resolved, owned, accepted or mitigated
  5. Confidence vote and final commitments

Root cause review (45 min)

  1. Timeline: what happened and when
  2. Customer impact and how we communicated it
  3. Root cause: ask "why" until you reach the real cause
  4. Fixes: each with an owner and a due date
  5. What we'll do differently next time (no blame)

Rules that make it work

  • Every meeting has an owner, an agenda and written action items.
  • If a meeting has nothing on the agenda, cancel it.
  • Status goes out in writing first, and meetings are for decisions.
  • Review the rhythm every quarter and drop meetings that no longer earn their time.
