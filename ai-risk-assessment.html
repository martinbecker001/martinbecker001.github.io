AI Risk Assessment Template

The method I use to review an AI system before and after it goes live

This is a general version of the method I use in the AI governance program I lead. The example at the bottom is a made-up system, so nothing here is confidential.

It follows the four functions of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. It also borrows from ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management) and the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.

Step 1 · Govern: who owns it?

  • Who is the system owner, and who approves it going live?
  • Which policies apply (AI use, data protection, security, vendor management)?
  • When will it be reviewed again (at least once a year, and after any major change)?

Step 2 · Map: what does it do?

  • Purpose: what problem does it solve, and for whom?
  • Users: employees, customers or the public?
  • Data: what goes in (personal data, customer data, source code) and where does it go?
  • Model: who provides the model, and is it hosted by us or by a vendor?
  • Autonomy: does it only answer questions, or can it take actions (send email, change records, run code)?
  • Human oversight: does a person review the output before it matters?

Step 3 · Measure: how risky is it?

Score each risk on likelihood (1–5) and impact (1–5), then multiply them.

ScoreLevelWhat it means
1–4LowAccept and watch
5–9MediumFix within 90 days
10–16HighFix within 60 days
17–25CriticalFix within 30 days, or don't launch

Use this checklist, based on the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025), to find risks:

#RiskQuestion to ask
1Prompt injectionCan someone trick it with hidden instructions in text it reads?
2Sensitive information disclosureCould it reveal personal, customer or secret data?
3Supply chainDo we trust the model, its vendor and the libraries it uses?
4Data and model poisoningCould bad data change how it behaves?
5Improper output handlingIs its output checked before another system uses it?
6Excessive agencyDoes it have more permissions than it needs?
7System prompt leakageAre there secrets in its instructions that could leak?
8Vector and embedding weaknessesIf it searches documents, can users see documents they shouldn't?
9MisinformationWhat happens if it is confidently wrong?
10Unbounded consumptionAre there limits on usage and cost?

Step 4 · Manage: what are we doing about it?

Every risk at Medium or above gets a fix, an owner and a due date in a remediation tracker. A second reviewer (risk, compliance or security) checks the evidence before a fix is marked done.

Example: a made-up customer support chatbot

"HelpBot" answers customer questions using the product documentation. It can open support tickets but can't issue refunds.
