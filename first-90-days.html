My First 90 Days

How I start as a Chief of Staff or senior program manager

Every company is different, but my approach is the same. First I listen, then I set up a steady rhythm, then I deliver results people can see.

Days 1–30 · Listen and learn

  • Meet one-on-one with my leader, each of their direct reports, and key peers in Product, Sales, Finance and Security.
  • Ask each person three questions: What's working? What's broken? What would you fix first?
  • Read the plans, roadmaps, metrics and recent incident reports.
  • Sit in on existing meetings without changing them yet.

Deliverable: a one-page "what I heard" summary for my leader, with the top 3 problems and where everyone agrees.

Days 31–60 · Set the rhythm

  • Agree with my leader on the top 3 priorities and what "done" looks like for each.
  • Set up a simple operating rhythm: weekly status, monthly planning and a quarterly review. (See my operating rhythm kit.)
  • Start one weekly status report for executives: what's on track, what's at risk, and what I need from them.
  • Pick a quick win (a broken process people complain about) and fix it.

Deliverable: a published meeting calendar, the first weekly executive status report and one quick win.

Days 61–90 · Deliver and measure

  • Run the first quarterly planning cycle with the new rhythm.
  • Set 3–5 measures of success (for example communication frequency, communication accuracy, scope change, say / do ratio).
  • Put an owner and a due date on every open action item.
  • Ask for feedback: what's better, and what still gets in the way?

Deliverable: a 90-day review with my leader covering results, metrics and the plan for the next quarter.

What I bring to it

I've started in new organizations at Cisco, GE, Oracle, Ontrak Health and SnapLogic. At GE, I joined a new division of 8 vice presidents who were heading in different directions. I documented each person's ideas, fit them into one strategy and ran the master plan that kept everyone aligned.
